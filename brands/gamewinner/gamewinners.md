# Game Winner — Etsy Studio Strategy

## The focused Etsy "studio" approach

A focused Etsy shop where the customer lands and sees nothing except:

- Dad scores the winning goal for England
- Husband hits a World-Series-style walk-off
- Boyfriend sinks the championship buzzer beater
- Grandad hits the winning six
- Brother scores the cup-final winner
- 50th birthday football version
- Father's Day football version
- retirement version
- baseball version
- basketball version

That feels much safer than landing in a shop that also sells tarot dogs, cereal boxes and album covers.

It says: **These people know how to make this particular thing look good.**

Because the customer is handing over a precious photo and doesn't know what the AI result will look like, **specialisation is itself a trust signal.**

Etsy explicitly says a second shop can make sense when the product line is substantially different. Multiple shops are allowed, although each needs its own Etsy account/email and you must disclose the other shops in each public profile. The catch: Etsy cannot transfer or combine shop data, reviews or sales between them. ([Etsy Help][1])

## Etsy supports the "meal deal"

Etsy now has **discounted bundles**. Up to three listings in one bundle; buyer gets the discount only when they buy all three. Etsy shows the bundle on the listing page and badges related items in cart. ([Etsy Help][3])

### THE HAT-TRICK — Buy all three → 15% off

**1. The Film — £7.99**
Dad scores the winner + commentary + crowd + downloadable portrait.

**2. Premium Mug / Beer Glass / Tumbler — £17.99**
Hero image + score/date/name.

**3. Personalised Birthday/Father's Day Card — £5.99**
Still from the winning moment, QR/access instructions for full video.

Etsy lets you track bundle performance including orders, units, AOV and revenue. ([Etsy Help][3])

## The customer proposition

The customer is not really buying "AI video."

She is thinking: "What the hell do I buy Dad/James/my husband this year?"

Game Winner says: **Put him in the moment he's imagined since he was eight years old.**

That's emotional **and** funny. Personalized without being soppy. Techy enough to feel novel. Something physical to unwrap. Everyone watches together. Demonstrates she actually knows what he likes.

### Football example

**90+3**

Final. 1–1. Dad comes on. Ball comes across.

**"AND IT'S STEVE PRIORRRRRRRRR!"**

Goal. Crowd explosion. Scoreboard. Kneeslide.

Commentator: "At fifty-eight years of age, Steve Prior has won it for his country!"

Then his son gives him the beer glass carrying the still from *that exact moment*.

That is an excellent birthday gift.

## The album studio parallel

Positioning: **Tell us about them. Not their résumé — the little things only you know.**

Ask:
- phrases they always say
- funny habits
- places
- important people
- pets
- formative era/music
- memories
- things the family teases them about

Output:
- album cover
- track listing where every title means something
- one actual finished song
- lyrics
- 15-second music video
- physical album-art gift

Feels handcrafted even though the system underneath is enormously automated.

A specialist store containing 50 examples of **people's lives turned into albums** feels much more credible than a generic AI personalization shop.

## Don't make human products sub-brands of MythicBee

MythicBee is a fantastic pet brand precisely because the name screams pets/art.

Putting "Game Winner — A MythicBee Brand" on a 60th birthday football gift creates unnecessary cognitive friction.

Instead, have an **invisible parent production company** eventually. Customers don't need to care.

Perhaps later:

**[Parent Studio]**
- MythicBee
- Game Winner
- Album…
- Oracle…

Say quietly "from the studio behind MythicBee" once MythicBee itself has meaningful social proof.

## One important Etsy limitation

Do **not** make the flow: Etsy → "buy it cheaper at MythicBee.com."

Etsy explicitly prohibits moving a transaction that began on Etsy off-platform, including directing buyers elsewhere to purchase and using QR codes for that purpose. ([Etsy][6])

Think: **TikTok / Google / Instagram → your own sites or Etsy**

Rather than using Etsy as free discovery and pushing shoppers out to your own checkout.

Fulfillment can obviously involve the web experience necessary to use the product (the personalized AR/video experience), but don't turn that into "buy your next product here to avoid Etsy."

Eventually standalone brands acquire their **own traffic**.

---

## The graduation rule

1. Build concept using shared infrastructure.
2. Create 5–10 high-quality examples.
3. Determine whether it represents one extremely obvious buying intent.
4. If yes, give it a focused Etsy studio.
5. Run 5–15 tightly related listings around recipient/occasion/search intent.
6. One flagship listing receives most optimization.
7. Once sales/reviews accumulate, **do not move it again**.

Moving a winner destroys accumulated shop/listing social proof. Etsy has no mechanism for transferring that history. ([Etsy Help][1])

## Game Winner is the first to skip the incubator

It's narrow enough to understand in one sentence but broad enough to produce dozens of listings and years of occasion variants.

**"We put the person you love into the sporting moment they've dreamed about their whole life."**

That's a business, not just an AI gimmick.
